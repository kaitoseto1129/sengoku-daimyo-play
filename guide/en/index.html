What is “Sengoku Daimyo”?

Sengoku Daimyo is a strategy game in which you lead one clan through the Warring States period — from Okehazama to the Siege of Osaka — in real time. You look over every castle in Japan on a map, enrich your provinces through domestic policy, send armies to besiege castles, and when a battle breaks out you move your units yourself. Nothing to install: it runs in a single browser page, including Safari on iPhone and iPad.

Start with the Battle of Nagashino

The scenario you can play for free is the Battle of Nagashino (1575–). You can play as Oda, Tokugawa, Takeda or any other clan in the realm. The world keeps going after Nagashino: Tennoji Fort, the Kizu river mouth, Tedorigawa, Araki Murashige's defection, the Otate Rebellion, Takatenjin, the Conquest of Kai, the Honnoji Incident… seven years of events follow one another. Other scenarios unlock with “Tenka Fubu” (an in-app unlock).

How to play — reading the screen

“Start Game” → choose a scenario. The free one is the Battle of Nagashino.

“Pick a clan on the map”. Tap a castle to see that clan's profile (difficulty, strengths, weaknesses). You can also pick from the list.

Time

Time keeps flowing unless you stop it. Press Ⅱ at the bottom right to pause and ×1×2×3 to change speed. When in doubt, pause. You can still open castle cards and councils while paused.

The castle card

Tap a castle to open its card. Koku, trade, garrison, castellan, people's morale and provisions are shown at a glance, and from here you run domestic affairs, conscription and marching.

  • Koku: rice yield. Tax = gold. Grows with land reclamation
  • Trade: the town's prosperity. Grows with investment and turns into gold
  • Garrison: total troops of the officers in the castle. Your defense when attacked
  • Castle level / defense: how strong the castle is. Fortify it to make it harder to take
  • People's morale: if it drops, uprisings and defections happen. “Alms” restores it
  • Provisions: food for the troops. When it runs out armies stop and morale drops

Crests identify clans

Every clan is represented by its crest (kamon) — on castle flags, army banners and battle standards alike. Your own crest is shown at the top left.

Domestic card

“Domestic” on the castle card: land reclamation, trade investment, fortification, alms and drills are assigned to idle officers. “Auto domestic” lets the castle run itself according to a policy.

Diplomacy

The diplomacy card: alliance, peace, submission and gifts. Each clan's crest and relationship value are listed. A vassal and an ally cannot be the same clan.

Opening — your first 30 days as Oda

If this is your first game, play Oda: many troops, plenty of gold, and at Nagashino you are on the defending (favored) side.

Order of business

  1. Pause first. Press Ⅱ, look at the map and find your castles and the enemy (Takeda)
  2. Open your home castle's card and check provisions and gold. If you have less than half a year of food, fix that before giving alms
  3. Auto domestic is fine. Choose “Auto domestic (delegate this castle)” on the card and idle officers get work according to the policy. Early on, all castles can run on auto
  4. Go easy on conscription. More troops means less food. The decisive battle's troops are provided by the scenario
  5. Check diplomacy. Tokugawa is your ally (they come to Shitaragahara). Uesugi, Honganji and Mori easily end up at war with you — use peace and pacts so you don't open new fronts
  6. Wait for the day of battle. The top bar shows “Shitaragahara in N days”. When it comes, the council “Preparing Shitaragahara” opens
Tip: Don't attack early. Win at Nagashino and the Takeda veterans fall; then leave the east to Tokugawa and turn west. Scattering troops before the battle thins your relief force.

Playing as Takeda

Takeda is the attacking side and the harder choice. A frontal assault on the palisades loses. In the council “Deciding how to attack Shitaragahara”, either mass on the left (or right) wing and break one point, or avoid the battle and take Nagashino Castle. Cavalry stop at the palisades, so break the fences with ashigaru before sending in the horse.

Provisions: troops eat every day. An army marching out carries 120 days of food from the castle's stores, so sending too many leaves the castle starving. Check remaining days in “Finances” and aim for half a year.
Don't: conscript heavily while ignoring people's morale. When morale falls, uprisings break out and a castle can end up lordless.

Warfare

Sending an army

“March” on the castle card. Pick officers, then tap a destination. Provisions are drawn from the stores.

  • Destinations are castles connected by highway. You cannot pass through castles of clans you are not at war with (ally with them, or take the castles on the way first)
  • To besiege a castle bring twice the garrison. The AI will not attack with less (you can, but it costs)
  • Meeting an enemy army on the road gives a field battle; reaching a castle starts a siege. A siege wears down the castle's defense each day; when it reaches zero the castle falls
  • You can send relief to a besieged castle. Come with twice the besieging force to drive them off

Fighting a battle

  • Tap a unit to select it, tap a square to move, tap an enemy to attack. Drag with your finger and the unit follows the path you drew
  • Units do not move until ordered (they hold). Cavalry “Charge” to rush in
  • “All-out charge” sends everyone; “Retreat all” ends the battle and withdraws (you lose a few troops)
  • Break the enemy HQ (the commander's unit) or drive off every enemy to win
  • High ground is easy to defend and matchlocks reach further from it. Rivers, fords and marsh slow you down and cavalry cannot charge there
  • Rain dampens the fuses and matchlocks stop working
Beginners: when in doubt, press “Auto battle”. You can take command back at any time. Once you're comfortable, put matchlocks behind palisades, spears in front and cavalry on the flanks.

The Battle of Nagashino

Oda and Tokugawa (defending)

Choose one of three setups. If unsure, pick Historical. Open “Details” to fine-tune.

SetupWhat it doesGood for
HistoricalThree rows of palisades, matchlocks behind them, Sakai's detachment strikes TobigasuFirst-time players. Easiest to win
AggressiveThin palisades, push out with cavalry and samuraiThose who want it over quickly
DefensiveThick palisades, whittle them down with guns. A long fightThose who want to minimize losses
  • Fire from behind the palisade. Matchlocks shoot over the fence. Don't send them outside
  • When a fence breaks, put samurai or ashigaru into the gap. A “Breach” marker shows where cavalry are coming through
  • When the Takeda veterans (Yamagata, Baba, Naito) break, all of Takeda loses morale. Pursue units showing the “badly wounded” banner to finish them
  • When Katsuyori starts to withdraw, don't chase too far. Pursue with cavalry and samurai only

Sakai's detachment and Tobigasu Fort

In the historical setup, Sakai Tadatsugu's detachment circles along the top of the field to Tobigasu Fort behind the Takeda lines, breaks its fences and takes it. Once the fort falls, Takeda morale drops and their reserves never arrive. Playing as Takeda you can garrison the fort to stop this.

Torii Suneemon (the messenger from Nagashino Castle)

Playing from Nagashino Castle there is a scene where you run a messenger to Okazaki from the besieged castle. Stay out of the torch cones of the patrols, use grass and darkness. Get through and the castle hears that relief is coming and keeps its morale; get caught and morale falls.

Winning as Takeda (attacking)

  • In the council “Deciding how to attack Shitaragahara”, mass on the left or right wing where the fence is thin
  • Break the fence with ashigaru (a unit of 1,500 takes about a minute per fence). Then send cavalry through the gap
  • Don't put cavalry in front of the matchlocks. Rain silences the guns (weather is luck)
  • Don't lose the veterans. Pull back units that are “wounded and withdrawing”
  • If it can't be won, withdraw. Routes: main highway, mountain path, riverside, the long way round. Up to three rearguard units. Keep Katsuyori alive and the clan survives
  • You can also avoid the battle and take Nagashino Castle instead (the clan grumbles, but you keep your men)

Tips for winning

1. Don't open more fronts

Fight one enemy at a time. Keep the others quiet with peace, pacts and alliances. Attacking means declaring war.

2. Attack with double, relieve with double

Twice the garrison to besiege, twice the besiegers to relieve. Half measures only cost troops.

3. Half a year of provisions

Check rice before raising troops. Armies carry 120 days of food out of the stores.

4. Keep people's morale above 40

Uprisings are the main way to lose a castle. Alms are cheap insurance.

5. Ride the historical battles

The scripted battles (Nagashino, Tennoji, Tedorigawa, Kai) are easy on the winning side. The losing side is where you learn to withdraw.

6. Aim for the HQ

You don't have to kill everyone. Break the enemy commander's unit and you win. Send cavalry in from the flank.

7. High ground and palisades

Defend on high ground behind fences. Matchlocks behind, spears in front. Cavalry stop at fences.

8. Vassal or ally, not both

A clan that submitted only joins its overlord's wars. Vassals never propose alliances.

9. Sieges are about time

Defending a castle, you don't need to destroy the enemy: hold the main keep until the timer runs out. Put matchlocks on the walls, fire volleys at the crowd before the gate, fall back to the next bailey when a gate is about to break, and keep reserves for the breach.

FAQ

Where are saves kept?

In that device's browser. They can't be moved to another device. On iPhone, “Add to Home Screen” makes them less likely to be cleared.

What about the other scenarios (Okehazama, Honnoji, Sekigahara…)?

They unlock with “Tenka Fubu”. Even continuing the Nagashino world, after the Honnoji Incident the events of Yamazaki, the Kiyosu conference, Shizugatake, Komaki-Nagakute, Shikoku and Kyushu happen depending on your clan.

Do my units move on their own in battle?

No. They hold until ordered. Command is delegated only when you press “Auto battle” (or delegate a wing).

Can I play on a phone?

Yes. Landscape is recommended. Pinch to zoom the map and drag to pan.

Is the whole game in English?

The interface — menus, castle cards, battle controls, councils and the HUD — is in English. Event narration and officers' lines are still in Japanese for now; officer and clan names are romanized.
